Three different apps solved the same problem — a plain timer doesn't hold
anyone's attention for long — by attaching a living thing to it. Forest grows
a tree. Finch raises a bird. PetPomo keeps a cat. They are not the same app
wearing different skins, and picking between them by vibes alone means
picking the wrong one for what you actually need.

## What each one is actually for

**Forest** is built around one mechanic: leave the app to touch your phone,
and the tree you planted dies. It is a commitment device first and a
timer second — the tree is a stake, not a companion. It is mobile-only, with
no web app, which matters if most of your distraction happens on a laptop
rather than a phone.

**Finch** is framed as self-care, not focus. Your bird only grows when you
complete wellness tasks — sleep, hydration, a two-minute journal entry, a
short walk — and sessions are optional rather than central. It has reportedly
built a substantial business on this model, which is real evidence the
underlying idea works; it is also account-required and mobile-only, and the
tasks are about you generally, not the thing you're trying to finish right
now.

**PetPomo** puts the timer at the center and the pet around it. Press Start,
the cat sleeps for the length of the session, and the session itself — not a
separate checklist — is what the relationship is built from. It runs in a
browser, needs no account, and has no subscription tier to be upsold into.

## Side by side

| | Forest | Finch | PetPomo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform | Mobile only | Mobile only | Any browser |
| Account required | No | Yes | No |
| Cost | Free, paid version available | Free, subscription tier | Free |
| What grows/reacts | A tree (dies if you leave the app) | A bird (grows via self-care tasks) | A cat (reacts to focus sessions directly) |
| Framing | Commitment device against phone use | Self-care and wellness | Focus companion |
| Works offline | Partial | No | Yes |

## Where each one genuinely wins

Being honest about the gaps matters more than winning every row. Forest's
phone-lockout mechanic is the most direct anti-distraction tool of the
three — nothing about PetPomo stops you opening another tab. Finch's
self-care framing reaches people for whom "be productive" is the wrong ask
entirely, and its polish and mobile-native design are real strengths.

What PetPomo is built for is the person who wants the companion mechanic
[without an account gate](/why-no-login-no-subscription/) and without the
whole thing tied to a phone they might be trying to put down in the first
place. If the problem is genuinely "I open my phone too much," Forest solves
that more directly than either of the other two.

## The idea underneath all three

None of this is a fad specific to any one app. There's a real, if
under-researched, [body of psychology behind why caring for something helps
you focus](/virtual-pet-psychology-focus/) at all — the three apps above are
different bets on the same underlying mechanism, not competing claims about
whether it's real.

Try whichever one matches how you actually work: phone-first and
distraction-led, wellness-first and task-agnostic, or timer-first with a
companion built around the actual sessions.
