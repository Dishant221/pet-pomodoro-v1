Coming back after a bad week is harder than the bad week was, because the
bad week only asked you to survive it — the return asks you to face what
lapsed. The single most useful thing to know is that re-entry is a separate
skill from discipline, and it is mostly about making the first step back
cost as little as possible.

## The return is the actual hard part

Bad weeks happen to everyone and need no instructions. Illness, grief, a
deadline that ate everything else, or the greyer kind of week where nothing
was wrong exactly and nothing got done either. The week itself is not the
problem to solve.

The problem is Monday after. You know what is waiting: the inbox with its
silt, the to-do list frozen mid-sentence like Pompeii, the routine that now
has a hole in it shaped like seven days. And so the avoidable thing happens —
you avoid it. Tuesday is a slightly worse Monday. The gap grows its own
gravity, and a bad week quietly applies for an extension.

Nobody drifts for a month because they enjoyed it. They drift because every
day away makes the door heavier, and nobody taught them the trick of doors.

## Most tools greet you with an audit

Here is where the tools you chose in a good mood betray you in a bad one.
Open the average productivity app after a week away and it leads with the
damage: the broken streak, the flatlined graph, the seventeen overdue items
in accusatory red. Every one of those is technically accurate, and all of it
answers a question nobody returning asks. You did not open the app for a
verdict. You opened it to come back.

This is not a small design detail — it is
[a large part of why productivity apps get abandoned](/why-productivity-apps-get-abandoned/).
An app that punishes returns trains you not to return. The streak was
supposed to be the reason you stayed; once broken, it becomes the reason you
never come back, because the first screen after a lapse is an inspection you
can fail.

Compare the way a dog greets you after a week away: no ledger, no
disappointment arithmetic — you are here, and here is good. When we built
PetPomo the rule was to copy the dog, not the auditor. The cat that was sad
while you were gone is simply pleased now that you are back. No streak
existed, so none broke; nothing you earned was taken away. The record shows
a quiet week and moves on, the way a decent colleague would.

## Make re-entry cheap on purpose

Whatever tools you use, you can lower the cost of the door yourself. The
principle is always the same: shrink the first step until it is smaller than
your resistance.

**Do not resume — restart small.** Resuming means facing everything where it
fell. Restarting means picking one thing. Not the most urgent thing, ideally
the *easiest* thing, because the goal of the first session back is not
output. It is proof that the door opens.

**One session is the whole assignment.** Twenty-five minutes, one task, and
then you are allowed to stop with a clear conscience. The finished feeling —
a rung bell, a closed loop — matters more on the first day back than on any
other day, which is a special case of
[why finishing beats accumulating](/finishing-what-you-start/).

**Let something be glad before you are.** On the worst days, the reason to
open the door cannot be the work, because the work is the thing you dread.
It can be something that missed you.
[Looking after a small creature](/why-caring-for-something-helps/)
puts a greeting on the other side of the door — you are not returning to an
audit, you are returning to something. Many people find that is the
difference between opening the app and circling it for another day.

## One session counts as coming back

Say it plainly, because the ambitious part of you will argue: the comeback
is not a heroic week. It is one ordinary session, completed, today. That is
the full definition. Everything after it is just more days.

The ambitious plan — up at six, inbox zero by nine, the lost week repaid by
Friday — feels like commitment and functions as a trap. It sets a bar high
enough to fail, and failing on day one of the return sends you back outside
the door, now with evidence. Repaying lost time is not a thing; the time is
spent. The only live question is whether today contains one finished block,
and that question is winnable before lunch.

## Do not relitigate the week

One more thing, about the story you tell afterwards. There is a strong pull
to hold an inquiry — why it happened, what it says about you, whether you
are, deep down, someone this always happens to. The inquiry feels like
responsibility. It is usually just the bad week continuing by other means.

A kinder and more accurate frame: weeks like that are weather, and you have
now walked back inside. If the timer helps you do that, [it is here](/), and
it will not ask where you have been. Start one small session, feed the cat
at the bell, and let that be the whole comeback. It counts. It always
counted.

PetPomo is a timer, not a treatment. Nothing here is medical advice — if the
bad weeks are frequent, or low mood is weighing on you, a doctor or a
specialist is the right person to talk to, and this page is no substitute.
